Self-healing: …

Switch it off to see what happens to the same failures without protection. Use it for the before and after comparison.

Crash the app

Kills the app process instantly, like a program crashing or running out of memory. The page goes offline for a moment. The watchdog, a separate program that checks the app every 2 seconds, sees that it has stopped and starts it again within a few seconds. Nothing you saved is lost.

Freeze the app

The app stays running but stops answering, like a program stuck in an endless loop. This is harder to spot than a crash because the process is still alive. The watchdog asks the app if it is well every 2 seconds. After 3 unanswered checks in a row it stops the frozen app and starts a fresh one, in about 8 to 12 seconds.

SMS service

Takes the reminder service offline. With self-healing on, the circuit breaker stops calling it and the app reconnects it automatically after about 10 seconds. With self-healing off it stays down until you run python chaos.py restore, or switch self-healing back on.

Internet connection

Cuts the link to the caregiver cloud. Take a dose on the Tracker page while it's cut. With self-healing on, doses are saved on this device and the app reconnects the link automatically after about 8 seconds. With self-healing off, doses are lost and the link stays cut until python chaos.py restore or until self-healing is switched back on.

Corrupt the database

Slips one deliberately broken record (blank name, dose -5, time 25:99) into the database, like bad data from a faulty import. It is never a real medicine and is never shown in your list. The app finds it within about 3 seconds and sets it aside in quarantine. Watch the Health page.

Wipe all data (can't be healed)

Deletes every medicine and all history. The app notices the loss and raises the alarm, but there is no backup, so it cannot get the data back. This is the limit we are honest about. It really deletes your data.
